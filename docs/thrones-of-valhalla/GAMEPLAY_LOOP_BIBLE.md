# Thrones Of Valhalla Gameplay Loop Bible

## Purpose

Thrones of Valhalla is a persistent Valheim kingdom server inspired by Game of Thrones. Normal Valheim play is the foundation: exploration, gathering, building, sailing, fighting, crafting, survival, and boss progression should still feel like Valheim.

The Thrones layer adds long-term realm identity. Houses rise, split, ally, betray, compete, support the Crown, challenge bosses through Royal Hunts, win tournaments, earn achievements, hold titles, and become part of server history.

The server is not hardcore roleplay and not hardcore PvP. The design should support rivalry and story without turning routine play into paperwork or making players feel punished for living normal lives outside the server.

## Design Pillars

- Valheim comes first.
- The Realm persists with no planned wipes.
- The Raven is the Realm's historian, not an auditor.
- The Raven should create stories, not paperwork.
- Progression should reward cooperation, exploration, courage, and memorable moments.
- Players should never need daily check-ins to remain relevant.
- Missing a week should not punish a player or make them feel permanently behind.
- Tracking should be automatic, one-click, simple Discord interaction, or rare staff confirmation.
- Frequent manual tracking from Chris, moderators, or players should be rejected unless it is optional flavor.
- Bosses are earned through Realm progression.
- Boss altars are physically sealed by the Crown until the Realm earns the right to challenge them.
- The Crown or its representative unlocks boss altars for scheduled Royal Hunts.
- Achievements are permanent records; titles are social recognition and may change.
- The server should not require more than about 15 players online at once.
- Houses should be flexible in number and size. Large Houses can naturally split into cadet branches or allied Houses.

## Core Gameplay Loop

The core loop should stay simple:

1. Players join the Realm and find a House.
2. Houses build, gather, explore, recruit, trade, compete, and develop identity.
3. The Realm advances toward the next Age through broad progress and Royal Tribute.
4. The Crown declares a Royal Hunt when the Realm earns a boss challenge.
5. The Realm gathers for the Hunt, defeats the boss, and unlocks the next Age.
6. The Raven records major events, achievements, titles, decrees, wars, builds, and succession moments.
7. The Realm continues without a planned wipe.

The system should create a steady rhythm of optional goals and major shared moments without converting Valheim into a checklist game.

## Player Journey

### First Login

A new player arrives as a newcomer to the Realm. Their first job is to learn the rules, reach King's Landing, understand the current Age, and choose a House path.

Expected early experience:

- Learn basic server rules and expectations.
- Travel to King's Landing.
- Swear allegiance to one House.
- Receive House approval.
- Receive Crown recognition.
- Begin normal Valheim progression with social context.

### Settled Member

Once accepted, a player contributes through normal play. They may gather, build, scout, fight, trade, sail, join tournaments, submit Chronicle stories, or support Royal Tribute.

The system should recognize meaningful participation without measuring every deposit, death, wall, or resource stack.

### Known Figure

Players become known through repeated visible actions:

- Helping a House grow.
- Building important settlements.
- Winning tournaments.
- Supporting Royal Hunts.
- Discovering meaningful places.
- Taking part in wars, alliances, betrayals, or succession events.
- Earning achievements or titles.

### Legend

A legend is a player whose actions become part of permanent history. This may happen through a boss kill, founding a House, building a landmark, winning a major tournament, leading a rebellion, defending the Realm, betraying the Crown, or shaping succession.

Legend status should emerge from memorable moments, not daily grind.

## House Loop

Houses are the primary social structure of the Realm.

The House loop:

1. Recruit and accept members.
2. Build and maintain a settlement.
3. Develop a banner, identity, leadership, and reputation.
4. Gather and prepare for Age goals.
5. Choose allies, rivals, and political positions.
6. Participate in Royal Tribute, Royal Hunts, tournaments, and wars.
7. Submit light weekly check-ins at most.
8. Split into branches or allied Houses if size or story makes that natural.

House leadership is chosen internally. The Crown recognizes Houses and may resolve disputes, but should not micromanage day-to-day House decisions.

## Realm Loop

The Realm loop is the shared server progression above individual Houses.

The Realm advances when players and Houses collectively:

- Explore the current Age.
- Establish settlements and infrastructure.
- Prepare resources that make sense for the current Age.
- Complete Royal Tribute expectations.
- Resolve official milestones through Crown or staff confirmation.
- Challenge bosses in scheduled Royal Hunts.
- Record major victories, failures, betrayals, and decrees.

Realm progress should be visible and communal. It should not require exact accounting of every individual's contribution.

## Age Progression Loop

Each Age represents the Realm's current phase of Valheim progression.

Age progression should follow this pattern:

1. The Realm enters an Age after a boss defeat or launch milestone.
2. Players and Houses explore that Age's biome, gear, resources, enemies, and building possibilities.
3. The Crown announces broad objectives and Royal Tribute needs.
4. Houses contribute through normal play.
5. The Raven reports public progress and Chronicle-worthy moments.
6. Staff or the Crown confirms when the Realm is ready.
7. The Crown schedules the Royal Hunt.
8. The boss defeat opens the next Age.

Age gates should create shared anticipation, not artificial chores.

## Royal Tribute Loop

Royal Tribute is the Realm's cooperative offering before a boss challenge or major progression unlock.

Royal Tribute should be:

- Achievable.
- Age-appropriate.
- Cooperative.
- Publicly visible.
- Broadly tracked.
- Light enough that normal Valheim play remains the main activity.

Royal Tribute should not require perfect individual accounting. The preferred record is that a House or player supported the Tribute, not that a player donated an exact number of every item.

The loop:

1. Crown announces the Tribute requirement.
2. Houses and players contribute through normal play.
3. The Raven posts public progress when useful.
4. House leaders may confirm broad participation.
5. Staff or Crown confirms completion.
6. Completion unlocks scheduling for the Royal Hunt or other Realm milestone.

## Royal Hunt And Boss Unlock Loop

Bosses are Realm milestones, not private speedrun targets.

Boss altar rules:

- Boss altars are physically locked or sealed until the Crown declares the Royal Hunt.
- No House or player may challenge a boss early.
- Boss access is earned through Age progression and Royal Tribute.
- The Crown or a representative opens the altar for the scheduled Royal Hunt.
- Rule breakers may be punished in-lore as banishment or execution, meaning suspension or ban.

The Royal Hunt loop:

1. Realm completes the required Age and Tribute conditions.
2. Crown declares the Hunt and announces date, time, and access rules.
3. Houses prepare gear, food, ships, portals, and fighters.
4. The Crown or representative unlocks the altar.
5. The Realm challenges the boss.
6. The Raven records the result.
7. Achievements and titles may be awarded.
8. The next Age opens if the boss is defeated.

Royal Hunts should be major shared events, but progression must not depend on 20 or more players being online at once. The system should work well with roughly 15 players or fewer online.

## No-Wipe, Endgame, And Succession Loop

The Realm persists. There are no planned wipes.

After the final boss, the server should shift from survival progression into legacy, politics, construction, rivalry, and succession.

Endgame loop:

1. Final boss is defeated.
2. The Age of Succession begins.
3. Crown authority can be challenged through controlled event rules.
4. King's Landing can become vulnerable during approved succession events.
5. A new ruler may be crowned.
6. The Realm continues with its history intact.

Succession should create new stories without deleting the world that produced them.

## Weekly Rhythm

The weekly rhythm should be light.

Accepted weekly activity:

- Optional play sessions.
- House building, gathering, scouting, sailing, and preparation.
- Tournaments or smaller events when scheduled.
- A light House leader check-in at most.
- Raven prompts with buttons or short confirmation modals.
- Optional Chronicle story submissions.

Rejected weekly pressure:

- Daily required quests.
- Mandatory attendance.
- Daily House reports.
- Punishing players for missing a week.
- Exhaustive contribution logs.

## Monthly Rhythm

The monthly rhythm can support larger arcs.

Possible monthly moments:

- Royal Tribute completion.
- Royal Hunts.
- Tournaments.
- House recognition.
- Major decrees.
- Settlement spotlights.
- Alliance or rivalry summaries.
- Achievement and legacy recaps.

Monthly structure should help the Realm feel alive without forcing a rigid calendar.

## Rare Realm-Changing Moments

Rare moments deserve staff or Crown confirmation because they change the official history of the Realm.

Examples:

- Boss defeats.
- House founding, disbanding, or splitting.
- Major alliances and betrayals.
- Major wars.
- Crown decrees.
- Succession events.
- King's Landing vulnerability events.
- Banishment, suspension, or ban for major rule breaking.
- Landmark settlements or server-defining builds.

These should be recorded carefully and sparingly.

## Fun, Not Chores

The operating rule is simple: if a system creates more paperwork than stories, reject it.

Good systems make players say, "I want to be there for that." Bad systems make players say, "I forgot to fill out the form."

The Raven should reduce burden, surface meaningful moments, and preserve history. It should not create a second job for players, House leaders, moderators, or Chris.

## Friction Budget

Every proposed system should fit this budget.

### Ideal: Automatic

Use GameOps, server data, or Raven state when the event can be captured safely without player effort.

Examples:

- Server milestone status.
- Known boss defeat data when available.
- Achievement triggers from reliable telemetry.
- Read-only progress summaries.

### Good: One Click

Use a button, reaction, or simple confirmation when a human signal is needed.

Examples:

- House leader confirms weekly status.
- Player confirms participation in a Royal Hunt.
- Crown confirms a Tribute phase is complete.

### Acceptable: Short Slash Command Or Modal

Use a short command or modal for meaningful events that need context.

Examples:

- Submit a Chronicle story.
- Propose a major build for recognition.
- Report a House split.
- Submit tournament results.

### Rare: Admin Or Mod Command

Use staff commands for official changes to Realm history or enforcement.

Examples:

- Confirm boss defeat.
- Recognize a new House.
- Record a Crown decree.
- Apply suspension or banishment.

### Avoid

Avoid patterns that create routine burden:

- Repeated typing.
- Spreadsheets.
- Screenshots as proof for normal gameplay.
- Daily reporting.
- Forms for routine gameplay.
- Exact item-by-item contribution accounting.

## Rejected Patterns

- Daily required quests.
- Mandatory attendance.
- Exhaustive contribution tracking.
- Systems that require Chris to audit everything.
- Progression depending on 20 or more players online at once.
- Punishing players for missing a week.
- Requiring screenshots as normal proof.
- Requiring players to report routine gathering, building, or deaths.
- Turning House leadership into bookkeeping.

## Accepted Patterns

- Weekly House check-in.
- Optional Chronicle stories.
- Read-only Chronicle feed.
- Read-only achievement feed.
- Raven prompts with buttons.
- Short modals for meaningful events.
- Admin-confirmed Realm milestones.
- Realm-wide boss unlocks.
- Public Royal Tribute progress posts.
- House-level participation recognition.
- Permanent achievements for memorable milestones.
- Changeable titles for current social recognition.

## Phase 1 Raven MVP Translation

The first Raven implementation implied by this design should focus on read-only state, light submissions, and rare admin-confirmed milestones. It should not attempt exhaustive gameplay tracking.

Phase 1 capabilities:

- Realm status view.
- House status view.
- Optional Chronicle submission.
- Admin House recognition.
- Admin Royal Hunt scheduling.
- Admin Royal Hunt completion.
- Admin achievement/title award.
- Read-only Chronicle feed.
- Read-only Achievement feed.
- Weekly House leader check-in prompt.

These capabilities should preserve the friction budget: automatic when possible, one-click when enough, short forms only for meaningful context, and admin confirmation only for official Realm changes.
