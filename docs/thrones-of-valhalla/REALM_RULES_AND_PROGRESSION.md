# Realm Rules And Progression

## Purpose

This document defines the permanent gameplay rules for joining the Realm, operating Houses, respecting Crown authority, progressing through bosses, and entering endgame succession.

These rules are design guidance for the server and future Raven implementation. They do not create Discord commands yet.

## Joining The Realm

New players enter the Realm through King's Landing and House allegiance.

Joining flow:

1. New player travels to King's Landing.
2. New player learns the current rules, Age, and House options.
3. New player swears allegiance to one House.
4. House leadership approves the member.
5. Crown recognizes the allegiance.
6. Player begins normal Valheim play under that House.

Players may belong to one House at a time.

Changing Houses requires House and Crown approval. This protects House identity, avoids casual team-hopping, and gives betrayals or transfers enough weight to become story when appropriate.

## Houses

Houses are the main social and political units of the Realm.

A House should have:

- A leader.
- A settlement.
- A banner, identity, or name.
- Recognized membership.
- Some public relationship to the Crown and other Houses.

House leadership is chosen internally. The Crown recognizes Houses and may resolve disputes, but should not micromanage normal House operations.

Houses may:

- Recruit members.
- Build settlements.
- Create branches or cadet Houses.
- Form alliances.
- Betray allies within server boundaries.
- Compete in tournaments.
- Contribute to Royal Tribute.
- Take part in Royal Hunts.
- Participate in wars or succession events when approved.

Large Houses can split naturally into cadet branches or allied Houses when size, politics, or story makes that healthier.

## Alliances And Betrayal

Alliances are allowed. Betrayal is allowed within the boundaries of server rules.

Good betrayal creates story. Bad betrayal destroys trust in the server.

Allowed patterns:

- Political betrayal.
- Switching sides through approved House and Crown process.
- Breaking alliances as part of public conflict.
- Deception within event rules.
- Succession challenges.

Rejected patterns:

- Griefing.
- Harassment.
- Destruction of settlements outside approved events.
- Theft or sabotage that violates server rules.
- Using out-of-game pressure to force outcomes.

## Crown

The Crown is the authority structure that keeps Realm progression coherent.

The Crown oversees:

- Realm progression.
- Royal Tribute.
- Royal Hunts.
- Boss altar unlocking.
- House recognition.
- Disputes.
- Tournaments.
- Decrees.
- Succession events.
- Rule enforcement.

The Crown does not micromanage normal Valheim play. Players and Houses should still gather, build, explore, fight, sail, trade, and survive freely within the rules.

## Boss Progression

Boss progression is Realm progression.

Rules:

- Boss altars are physically locked or sealed until the Crown declares the Royal Hunt.
- No House or player may challenge a boss early.
- Boss access is earned through Realm and Age progression.
- Royal Tribute may be required before a boss challenge.
- When earned, the Crown or a representative opens the altar for the scheduled Royal Hunt.
- The boss result becomes part of Realm history.

Breaking boss rules is a serious offense. Players who bypass locks, summon early, exploit access, or otherwise violate boss progression may be punished in-lore as banishment or execution, meaning suspension or ban.

## Royal Tribute

Royal Tribute is the cooperative offering that proves the Realm is ready for a major unlock.

Royal Tribute should be:

- Achievable.
- Age-appropriate.
- Cooperative.
- Publicly understandable.
- Light enough to preserve normal Valheim play.
- Broadly tracked rather than perfectly itemized.

Royal Tribute should encourage Houses to work together without requiring exact individual accounting.

Good Tribute design:

- Uses resources that make sense for the current Valheim Age.
- Creates reasons to explore and cooperate.
- Can be completed by a healthy server population without requiring 20 or more players online at once.
- Allows Houses to contribute in different ways.
- Recognizes broad participation.

Bad Tribute design:

- Requires daily chores.
- Requires staff to inspect every chest.
- Requires players to submit screenshots for ordinary contributions.
- Punishes Houses for missing a week.
- Depends on perfect individual ledgers.

## Royal Hunts

A Royal Hunt is the official Realm challenge of a boss.

Royal Hunt flow:

1. Realm completes required Age and Tribute conditions.
2. Crown announces the Hunt.
3. Houses prepare.
4. Crown or representative unlocks the altar.
5. Players challenge the boss.
6. Raven records the result.
7. Achievements, titles, and Chronicle records may follow.
8. The next Age opens if the boss is defeated.

Royal Hunts should be memorable server moments. They should remain accessible to the active Realm population and should not require mandatory attendance.

## PvP And Rivalry Rules

Competition is encouraged. Griefing is prohibited.

Allowed rivalry:

- Tournaments.
- Political competition.
- Trade competition.
- Public challenges.
- Approved wars.
- Controlled succession events.
- Betrayal within boundaries.

Prohibited behavior:

- Griefing.
- Harassment.
- Unapproved settlement destruction.
- Spawn camping.
- Exploiting boss gates.
- Destroying public infrastructure without approval.
- Turning normal PvP into server damage.

Destroying settlements is prohibited unless it is part of an approved Realm event with clear rules.

## King's Landing

King's Landing is the central Crown location and primary new-player destination.

King's Landing should support:

- Onboarding.
- Crown recognition.
- Decrees.
- Royal Hunt announcements.
- Tournaments and ceremonies.
- Succession stakes when the endgame begins.

King's Landing should not be casually raidable or destructible during normal play. It may become vulnerable only under controlled event rules during the Age of Succession or other approved Realm-changing events.

## Rule Enforcement

Rule enforcement can be expressed in lore, but the consequence is real.

Examples:

- Banishment may mean suspension or removal from the server.
- Execution may mean a ban for severe violations.
- Loss of title may reflect social or political consequence.
- Crown sanction may restrict event participation.

The Raven may record major enforcement as Realm history when appropriate, but routine moderation details should not become public spectacle.

## Endgame And Succession

After the final boss, the Realm enters the Age of Succession.

Endgame principles:

- The Realm continues.
- No planned wipe follows final boss completion.
- Crown authority can be challenged.
- King's Landing can become vulnerable under controlled event rules.
- A new ruler can be crowned.
- Houses may rise, fall, ally, betray, or split.
- Achievements and Chronicle records preserve the legacy of the old order.

Succession should create new stakes without deleting old history.

## Progression Guardrails

Progression systems should be rejected if they:

- Require daily attendance.
- Require 20 or more players online at once.
- Require exhaustive manual tracking.
- Depend on Chris or moderators auditing routine play.
- Punish normal absence.
- Make ordinary Valheim feel secondary.

Progression systems should be accepted when they:

- Preserve Valheim as the core experience.
- Create memorable Realm moments.
- Use broad House and Realm participation.
- Keep confirmation simple.
- Let the Raven preserve history.
- Keep the server playable by a realistic active population.
